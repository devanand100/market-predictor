Two weekly Nifty option strategies that survived the out-of-sample test

Five years of DhanHQ 1-minute option, spot and India VIX data plus NSE participant OI were used to test six strategy families. Settings were chosen on 2021–2023 only and then run once on 2024–2026. Two held up after every charge, slippage and running cost: a non-directional weekly short strangle, and a directional credit spread that trades only when FII option positioning and the opening gap agree. The headline figures below are for the strangle; the spread has its own section.

Updated 1 Oct 2026. A backtest bug was fixed: strikes that moved outside the data window kept stale prices, which understated losses on big moves. All figures on this page are rerun on the fixed engine. Strategy 2 fell from 18.0% to 12.4% CAGR; Strategy 1 rose from 11.1% to 12.6%.

Strategy 2: directional spreads from NSE data, PCR and price action

Each week, 15 point-in-time signals were used to pick a direction and trade a vertical spread. The signals came from NSE participant OI (FII, Client, Pro), PCR from the option chain, price-action trend, and VIX. Every signal was then compared with 10,000 random direction choices over the same weeks. The p-value is the chance that random choices would have done as well.

Only two signals beat chance in both periods: the change in FII index-option positioning, and the Nifty opening gap on entry day. They are almost unrelated to each other (correlation 0.07). Trading only when both point the same way was significant in both periods (p = 0.011 each) and in all three spread types tested.

All three rebased to ₹5 lakh on 27 May 2022, the first week with enough FII history to centre the signal. Strategy 2 trades about 28 weeks a year and sits out the rest.

Strategy 2 rules

  1. FII signal: from the NSE participant OI file published the evening before entry, take FII index options (Call long − Call short) − (Put long − Put short), change it day over day, and subtract the median of past weeks. Above zero is bullish.
  2. Gap signal: Nifty at 09:30 on entry day vs the previous close. Up is bullish.
  3. Trade only if both agree. Bullish: sell a Put at ATM − ½ straddle and buy a Put 200 points lower. Bearish: sell a Call at ATM + ½ straddle and buy a Call 200 points higher.
  4. No stop. The spread caps the loss. Close at 15:15 on expiry day.
  5. Sizing: a full-loss week is capped at 8% of equity (200 pts × 65 × lots). That was about 4 lots on ₹5 lakh, with far less margin than a naked strangle.

How much to trust Strategy 2

  • These two signals were picked after seeing that they held their sign in both periods, so 2024–2026 is not fully unseen for this strategy the way it is for the strangle. Treat the results as strong evidence, not proof.
  • It has four years of data (119 trades), not five and a half. 2026 so far is −10.0%. The analysis below shows why.
  • The weekly returns of the two strategies are almost uncorrelated (0.16). Running both on split capital lowered the worst week from −8.8% to −6.6%.

What lost money

  • Mar–May 2024, before the election. VIX rose from 14 to 21 and the market chopped. A bull put and then a bear call were both hit; May 2024 was −9.1%.
  • 27 Sep 2024. A bull put at the market top, followed by the −3.7% week that began the FII selloff.
  • 9 May 2025. A bear call during the India–Pakistan conflict, hit by the +4% ceasefire rally.
  • Feb 2026. VIX of 11–12 meant a small straddle, so the short strike sat only 95–150 points away. Ordinary 1% dips caused near-max losses.
  • Mar 2026. Bull puts entered on gap-up bounces inside a steep fall (Nifty 3.6–3.9% below its 20-day average, VIX 19–25). They lost −7.1% and −5.0%.

What made money

  • Jun–Sep 2024. 11 straight bull put wins in a steady uptrend at VIX ~14.
  • Oct 2024 – May 2025. 15 straight wins, +28%, while FIIs sold. Two in three trades were bear calls.
  • Jun–Aug 2025. 9 straight wins at calm VIX 12–15.
  • In profitable months the direction was right 82% of the time, against 33% in losing months.
  • All 19 losing trades were wrong-direction calls on big moves (median 1.58% vs 0.85% for winners). Sideways weeks never lost.

Strategy 1 rules: weekly strangle

  1. Entry: 09:30, exactly 4 trading sessions before expiry. In a normal 5-session week that is the first session after the previous expiry. Weeks with fewer than 5 sessions (holidays) are skipped, which is why there are 216 trades, not about 294.
  2. Strikes: read the ATM straddle price S. Sell the Call at ATM + S and the Put at ATM − S, rounded to 50. S is the market's own estimate of the week's move.
  3. Stop: close both legs if the combined loss reaches 1.5× the premium collected. Checked every minute.
  4. Exit: otherwise close at 15:15 on expiry day. Never hold into settlement.
  5. Sizing: lots of 65. Margin is taken as about 9% of notional per strangle; use at most 60% of capital as margin and keep 40% as a buffer. Lots grow with equity. The backtest ran 2–3 lots on ₹5L.
  6. No FII, VIX or trend filter. None of them held up out-of-sample.

Before trading this with real money

  • The edge is thin. Doubling slippage cuts CAGR from 12.6% to 7.7%, so fill quality decides whether this is worth running.
  • Gap risk is not hedged. The option data starts in 2021, so no crash on the scale of March 2020 is in the test.
  • Paper trade for 8–12 weeks, compare your real fills with the slippage assumed here, then start at half size.
  • Below about ₹5 lakh, the fixed monthly costs eat most of the return.
  • F&O profit is taxed as business income. All returns shown are pre-tax.
